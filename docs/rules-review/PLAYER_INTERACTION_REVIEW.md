**Date:** 2026-11-23. **Owner direction:** The game must be **interactive between people**, not a parallel solo race. Interpret this as an approved **design goal** of consequential tactical interaction in a human multiplayer board game; **do not infer approval for online networking, real-time reaction turns or new implementation**. Current `main` offers local boardgame.io play; online lobby/multiplayer is not active. Separate multiplayer hosting/connectivity decisions remain on the roadmap.

## Interactive design principle

A multiplayer turn should normally offer opportunities to **affect or anticipate another human player**, while preserving one teachable action sequence: **choose one tactical option (card OR normal Drop In OR none) → perform the required legal move → evaluate draw**, subject to accepted rescue/action-slot constraints. Real engagement can arise from **contested route occupancy**, visible Stones and hazards, proximity-limited attacks, player-to-player card transfer after a knockdown, positional exchanges and advance protection. Avoid making every turn a mandatory confrontation or adding reaction interruptions to other people's turns without explicit owner decision.

### Candidate design package for the next model (not approved)

1. **Adjacent Drop In** already confirmed; keep experimental max-two-dice/no-damage tie, and trial strict loser −1 energy. On a strict **attacker win while both stand**, optionally exchange occupied edge-neighbor hexes; consume the normal maneuver. If either falls, **no exchange** and they **remain on the final occupied hex**, preserving SK-DEC-023. If attacker loses and remains upright, still take normal move; if they fall, no maneuver that own turn. Do not invent placement overlap or free extra attack.
2. **Direct attack cards** have proposed short graph ranges (Big Wave 2, Sunburn 3), as in B60 review; avoid remote attacks anywhere on the 53-cell board and test multi-player gang-up pressure before setting limits.
3. **Positional and obstacle cards** encourage interaction, not just private energy management: Stone blocks a shared lane, Jumping counters placed obstacles, Tsunami can repel rivals, Change contests position, Cyclone changes local routing, and Surf Current can approach but not jump an occupied opponent. Track **opponent decisions affected**, not just card-use count.
4. **Counterplay** is proactive: publicly visible Hang Loose/Amulet lasts until next owner turn in proposed fixtures. Opponent can redirect tactics or choose another route. No new instant reaction slot approved.
5. **Social fairness:** Track how many different opponents are attacked, repeated target frequency, group-gang-up effects on one player, who can steal/retain rescue cards, number of positional overtakes, and whether front-runners can be intercepted. Attackers receiving cards on fall can snowball. Separate human interaction goals from online networking.

## Recommended next validation gates and evidence to record

- **G1 — Integrated Drop In:** Compare damage-only vs optional swap under **the same 2/4/7-player seeded hands/positions**. Count strict wins, legal exchanges, prevented overtakes, times a defender falls and blocks their tile, and attacks that affect the beach finish. Ensure the attacker cannot swap after knocking someone down.
- **G2 — Displacement + hazard attribution:** Controlled Stone→Shark and Tsunami/Change→Shark cases with at least two attribution policies. Ask owner whether direct intentional forcing by a rival awards a card, versus hazard-only discard, without changing the one-loss-per-fall rule.
- **G3 — Recovery/defense and targeting:** Proactive Amulet/Hang Loose, finite protection duration, rescue cards lost to attackers, empty hands, attacker-initiated losses, and full-hand card receipts; reject unwarranted non-Shark immunity.
- **G4 — Pursuit and economy:** Measure Surf Current benefit by **current race position** (leader/trailer), not just global turns-to-finish; compare no card, unconditional extra step and a possible trailing-only variant. Monitor hand-cap/draw policy and cap on steals.
- **G5 — Human tabletop session:** Before implementation approval, play actual facilitated 2/4/7-player games with rules printed, log **meaningful opponent-targeted actions, choices, boredom/wait time, blocked turns, runaway leader events, tactical counterplay and perceived fairness**. Deterministic models cannot replace social feedback.

**No extra decisions accepted:** The owner's desire for an interactive player-to-player game is captured as a design objective. The optional swap, ranges, reaction/defense timing, speed card and attribution variants remain proposals. **No application source was modified; no Jest/build/UI/human-play tests, merge or deploy.**
